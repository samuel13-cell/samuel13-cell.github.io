The year India's coal turned

India's coal generation fell in 2025. It has fallen before, but never like this: every previous fall came in a year when the country used less electricity overall. This time demand grew and coal fell anyway.

The finding

Coal generation
-44 TWh against 2024, from 1518 to 1474
Solar and wind
+75 TWh added, more than the 45 TWh rise in total generation
Coal's share
70.8% Of all generation, down from a peak of 77.0% in 2016

That arithmetic is the whole story. Solar and wind added 75 TWh while total generation rose 45 TWh. Renewables covered all of the growth in demand and displaced 44 TWh of coal on top of it.

Coal generation has fallen in only three years since 2000. In 2020 it fell because the country used less electricity overall. In 2019 the fall was 0.1 TWh, a rounding margin on a base above a thousand. 2025 is the first year the fall is both large and unaccompanied by a drop in demand.

Coal against solar and wind

Coal's share of generation

The share falling and the output falling are different claims, and only the second is new. Coal's share has declined since 2016 simply because renewables grew faster from a small base. Output falling means coal plants ran less in absolute terms, which is what has to happen for emissions to fall.

Method

Our World in Data publishes annual electricity generation by source, compiled from Ember and the Energy Institute, as one CSV. This uses India from 2000 to 2025, in terawatt hours. Total is the sum of the eight sources published, so shares are of generation, not of capacity.

Generation rather than capacity is the deliberate choice. Capacity measures what has been built; generation measures what actually ran. For a grid where coal plants run most of the time and solar runs a quarter of it, the two tell different stories and only one of them is about electricity delivered.

What this does not show

One year is not a trend. A mild monsoon, a soft demand year or a good hydro season can move these numbers, and coal output could rise again in 2026 without anything structural having changed. The claim here is narrow: this particular year broke a pattern that had held since 2000.

Annual national totals also hide the things that decide whether the turn holds: what happens after dark, how much storage exists, and how much coal capacity sits idle but available. None of that is visible at this resolution.

Build notes

What this page is made of, and how to rebuild it. The source lives in pipeline/.

Source
Our World in Data, compiled from Ember and the Energy Institute. Chosen after India’s own portal proved unusable: data.gov.in disallows crawling in its robots.txt, and its API offers no search, so finding a dataset would have meant paging 287,810 records at 100 per request.
Fetch
owid_fetch.py, plain requests. The CSV is stored byte for byte with its sha256, so a parse change re-runs without a refetch and every number here traces back to a specific file.
Transform
grid_transform.py, DuckDB straight over the raw CSV. No database server and no ORM: the query is the analysis.
Render
Static HTML with inline SVG. No chart library, no build step and no JavaScript beyond the hover layer, so the page has nothing to rot.
Colour
The categorical blue, orange and violet, each validated against this page’s own surface for lightness, chroma, colour-blind separation and contrast rather than assumed to carry over. Every green candidate for a third series failed: all of them collided with the orange under protanopia.

Pipeline

  1. Download the generation CSV and record url, size and sha256 in a manifest.
  2. Load it into DuckDB and filter to India from 2000, keeping the eight published sources.
  3. Derive a combined solar-and-wind series and a total, so shares are of generation rather than of installed capacity.
  4. Compute every year-on-year change in coal with a window function, which is what turns “coal fell” from an impression into a checkable claim.
  5. Write a JSON payload plus a CSV, then render the page from the payload.

The query the headline rests on

SELECT Year,
       ROUND(coal  - LAG(coal)  OVER (ORDER BY Year), 2) AS d_coal,
       ROUND(total - LAG(total) OVER (ORDER BY Year), 2) AS d_total
FROM g
QUALIFY d_coal < 0
ORDER BY Year;

-- Year   d_coal   d_total
-- 2019    -0.10    +29.33
-- 2020   -54.98    -37.55
-- 2025   -43.75    +45.08

What was checked

  • Every prior year in which coal fell, not only the latest. An early draft of this page claimed 2025 was the first fall, which the data does not support.
  • The 2019 fall is 0.10 TWh against a base of 1,199, a rounding margin rather than a turn.
  • The 2020 fall came with total generation falling 37.55 TWh, so coal fell because everything fell.
  • Totals are summed from the eight published sources rather than read from a total column, so the parts and the whole cannot disagree.
