The Impact Player effect

In 2023 the IPL let each side swap in a substitute mid-match. Scoring jumped immediately. The interesting question is not whether it rose. It is which part of the innings moved, because that points at why.

The finding

+1.70 Runs per over, overs 1–6, before the rule against after
Death overs
+0.74 Overs 16–20, already the fastest phase, and it moved least
Totals over 200
51% Share of first innings passing 200 in 2026, against 18% in 2022

If batters had simply got stronger, through better bats, shorter boundaries and the general drift of T20, then the death overs should have risen most. That is where sides already swing hardest, so extra power shows up there first. Instead the powerplay rose most, by +1.70 runs an over against +0.74 at the death.

The powerplay went from the slowest phase of an IPL innings to nearly the fastest. That is not a change in how hard players hit. It is a change in how much risk they are willing to take early, which is exactly what having an extra batter in reserve buys you.

Method

Cricsheet publishes every delivery of every IPL match as open data. This uses all 295,732 of them across 1,243 matches, 2008 to 2026, aggregated in DuckDB. Phases follow the competition's own structure: the six-over powerplay, the middle overs, and the last five. Runs per over counts every run conceded, byes and wides included, since those are runs the fielding side gave away.

“Before” is every season up to 2022 and “after” is 2023 onward. The rule applied from the first match of 2023, so the split needs no judgement call.

What this does not show

This is an association in observational data, not a controlled experiment. The rule was not assigned at random and 2023 changed more than one thing at once: squads turn over, new players arrive, grounds and conditions vary by season. A general rise in T20 batting standards is real and is part of this.

What the phase breakdown does is narrow the field. A pure power story predicts the death overs move most; they moved least. That is evidence for a change in risk-taking rather than in hitting ability. It remains evidence, not proof, and one competition over four seasons is a small sample to hang a causal claim on.

Build notes

What this page is made of, and how to rebuild it. The source lives in pipeline/.

Cricsheet, which publishes every delivery of every IPL match as open data. 295,732 rows across 1,243 matches.
Fetch
ipl_fetch.py downloads the archive and extracts only the consolidated file; the per-match CSVs hold the same rows.
Transform
ipl_transform.py, DuckDB. Phases are derived from the over number rather than hard-coded per season, so the split holds if the format changes.
Render
Static HTML with inline SVG. No chart library, no build step and no JavaScript beyond the hover layer, so the page has nothing to rot.
Colour
The categorical blue, orange and violet, each validated against this page’s own surface for lightness, chroma, colour-blind separation and contrast rather than assumed to carry over. Every green candidate for a third series failed: all of them collided with the orange under protanopia.

Pipeline

  1. Download the ball-by-ball archive and record its sha256.
  2. Derive a phase for every delivery from its over: powerplay, middle, death.
  3. Key seasons by the calendar year of their first match, since Cricsheet labels some as 2007/08 and one as 2020/21.
  4. Aggregate runs, boundaries, wickets and first-innings totals per season and per phase.
  5. Split before and after the rule year and compare phase by phase.

Deriving the phase, and the season key

SELECT *,
       CASE WHEN FLOOR(ball) < 6  THEN 'powerplay'
            WHEN FLOOR(ball) < 15 THEN 'middle'
            ELSE 'death' END              AS phase,
       runs_off_bat + extras              AS runs
FROM read_csv_auto('all_matches.csv');

-- Cricsheet labels seasons '2007/08' and '2020/21'; the calendar
-- year of the first match is the one unambiguous key.
SELECT season, CAST(strftime(MIN(start_date), '%Y') AS INT) AS yr
FROM b GROUP BY season;

What was checked

  • The phase split is computed from the data, so an over misfiled in the source lands in the right bucket rather than a hard-coded range.
  • Season keys were checked against the mixed labelling before use; naive sorting of “2007/08” against “2009” would have reordered the series.
  • Runs per over counts extras as well as runs off the bat, because those are runs the fielding side conceded.
  • The alternative explanation was tested, not waved away: a pure hitting-power story predicts the death overs move most, and they moved least.
