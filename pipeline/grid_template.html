The year India's coal turned

India's coal generation fell in @@last@@. It has fallen before, but never like this: every previous fall came in a year when the country used less electricity overall. This time demand grew and coal fell anyway.

Method

Our World in Data publishes annual electricity generation by source, compiled from Ember and the Energy Institute, as one CSV. This uses India from @@first@@ to @@last@@, in terawatt hours. Total is the sum of the eight sources published, so shares are of generation, not of capacity.

Generation rather than capacity is the deliberate choice. Capacity measures what has been built; generation measures what actually ran. For a grid where coal plants run most of the time and solar runs a quarter of it, the two tell different stories and only one of them is about electricity delivered.

What this does not show

One year is not a trend. A mild monsoon, a soft demand year or a good hydro season can move these numbers, and coal output could rise again in @@next@@ without anything structural having changed. The claim here is narrow: this particular year broke a pattern that had held since @@first@@.

Annual national totals also hide the things that decide whether the turn holds: what happens after dark, how much storage exists, and how much coal capacity sits idle but available. None of that is visible at this resolution.
