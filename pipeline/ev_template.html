Where the @@last@@ figures sit

The countries at the top are small, wealthy and cold, with dense charging networks and, in most cases, years of purchase subsidies behind them. That is the honest reason for the gap: India's car market is price-led in a way Norway's is not, and an electric car still carries a purchase premium that most Indian buyers will not pay without a subsidy.

Method

Our World in Data publishes the share of new car sales that are electric, compiled from the IEA's Global EV Outlook, as a single CSV covering @@first@@ to @@last@@. Electric here means battery electric and plug-in hybrid together, which is the IEA's definition.

The ranking counts only entities carrying a country code, which excludes aggregates such as the world average and regional groupings. Countries that did not report a @@last@@ figure are absent from the ranking rather than counted as zero.

What this does not show

This is new cars only. It says nothing about two and three wheelers, which is where India's electrification is actually happening at scale, nor about buses. A country can look slow on this measure while moving quickly on the vehicles its people mostly buy, and India is the clearest example of exactly that.

It is also a share, not a count. India's car market is large enough that @@india_now@@% of it is a substantial number of vehicles, and a high share of a small market can be fewer cars than a low share of a big one.
