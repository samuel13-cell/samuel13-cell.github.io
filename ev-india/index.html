India's electric car problem

The share of new cars sold in India that are electric nearly doubled in 2025. It is still 4.0%, against a world average of 25%. Both of those facts are true and most coverage picks one.

The finding

India, 2025
4.0% Of new cars sold, up from 2.1% in 2024
World
25% More than six times India's share
Rank
55 of 59 Countries with reported 2025 figures, ordered by share

Growth rates flatter small numbers. Doubling from 2.1% to 4.0% is a real acceleration, and it is also the kind of move that is easy to make from a low base. The level matters more than the rate here: on share of new sales, India ranks 55 of 59 countries reporting a 2025 figure.

India against the markets it is usually compared to

Where the 2025 figures sit

The countries at the top are small, wealthy and cold, with dense charging networks and, in most cases, years of purchase subsidies behind them. That is the honest reason for the gap: India's car market is price-led in a way Norway's is not, and an electric car still carries a purchase premium that most Indian buyers will not pay without a subsidy.

Method

Our World in Data publishes the share of new car sales that are electric, compiled from the IEA's Global EV Outlook, as a single CSV covering 2010 to 2025. Electric here means battery electric and plug-in hybrid together, which is the IEA's definition.

The ranking counts only entities carrying a country code, which excludes aggregates such as the world average and regional groupings. Countries that did not report a 2025 figure are absent from the ranking rather than counted as zero.

What this does not show

This is new cars only. It says nothing about two and three wheelers, which is where India's electrification is actually happening at scale, nor about buses. A country can look slow on this measure while moving quickly on the vehicles its people mostly buy, and India is the clearest example of exactly that.

It is also a share, not a count. India's car market is large enough that 4.0% of it is a substantial number of vehicles, and a high share of a small market can be fewer cars than a low share of a big one.

Build notes

What this page is made of, and how to rebuild it. The source lives in pipeline/.

Source
Our World in Data, compiled from the IEA Global EV Outlook. One CSV, no key, no crawl restriction.
Fetch
owid_fetch.py pulls this alongside the generation series, sharing one manifest.
Transform
ev_transform.py, DuckDB. The ranking counts only entities carrying a country code, which is what excludes aggregates like the world average from a list of countries.
Render
Static HTML with inline SVG. No chart library, no build step and no JavaScript beyond the hover layer, so the page has nothing to rot.
Colour
An emphasis chart rather than a categorical one: India carries the accent and the comparison markets recede to a neutral. When one series is the point, colouring all four equally buries it.

Pipeline

  1. Download the EV share CSV and record its sha256.
  2. Build a year axis from the focus country’s own first year, so the chart does not open on a decade of flat lines.
  3. Read each comparison market into the same axis, leaving gaps where a country has no figure rather than filling them with zero.
  4. Rank the latest year across countries only, then locate India within it.
  5. Render the trend as an emphasis chart and the ranking as horizontal bars.

Excluding aggregates from a ranking of countries

SELECT Entity, ROUND(share, 1) AS share
FROM e
WHERE Year = 2025
  AND Code IS NOT NULL
  -- OWID gives every aggregate a synthetic OWID_ code: World, Europe,
  -- EU27, the income groups. Kosovo is the one real place with one.
  AND (Code NOT LIKE 'OWID_%' OR Code = 'OWID_KOS')
ORDER BY share DESC;

What was checked

  • Aggregates are absent from the ranking. An earlier version excluded only the world total, which let Europe and the EU27 rank as countries above India; the published rank was 57th of 61 until that was caught. It is 55th of 59.
  • Countries with no figure for the latest year are absent rather than counted as zero, which would have flattered India’s rank.
  • Gaps in a series break the line instead of being drawn through, so a country that starts reporting late does not appear to have been at zero.
  • The claim is about share of new car sales, and the page says so plainly: two and three wheelers are where India’s electrification actually is.
