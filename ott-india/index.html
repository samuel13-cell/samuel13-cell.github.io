India watches the world

Netflix publishes its own weekly Top 10 for every market it operates in. Five years of it, for India and 92 other countries, answers a question the industry argues about: how much of what India watches is actually made for India?

Nearly half of India's Top 10 slots go to titles that chart in more than fifty countries. These are global releases that India happens to watch at the same time as everyone else. Titles that chart only in India account for 1.9% of slots.

That is not how every market behaves. Japan and South Korea fill roughly four slots in ten with titles almost nobody outside their region watches. India, on this measure, sits closer to Brazil or Spain than to its Asian neighbours.

How locally anchored each market is

The local share is shrinking

The trend runs against the industry's stated direction. Streaming services have spent these years commissioning more local originals, not fewer. On this measure the Indian Top 10 has become steadily more global anyway, roughly halving its local share since 2023.

Films are the exception worth noting. Indian film slots are 11.5% locally anchored against 2.3% for TV, and the average Indian film in the Top 10 reaches 35 countries against 59 for TV. Whatever local commissioning is achieving, it is holding ground in film and losing it in series.

Method

Netflix publishes a Top 10 for each country each week. This uses every Indian row from 2021-07-04 to 2026-09-06: 5,420 slots covering 1,383 distinct titles, alongside the same data for 92 other markets, aggregated in DuckDB.

The data carries no language or country-of-origin field. Rather than guess one from title text, a title's reach is counted directly: the number of distinct countries in which it ever enters a Top 10. Five or fewer is treated as locally anchored. The threshold is a choice, but the pattern is not sensitive to it: India sits far below Japan and Korea whether the line is drawn at three countries or ten.

What this does not show

A Top 10 is a ranking, not a viewing figure. It says a title was among the ten most watched that week, not how many people watched it, so a market where one title dominates and one where ten titles are close look identical here.

It also covers one service. Netflix is a minority of Indian streaming, well behind JioHotstar and Amazon Prime Video, and its subscriber base skews urban, English-comfortable and affluent. The honest reading is that this describes what Netflix India's audience watches, which is not the same as what India watches.
